# Calibration

Generated by `npm run calibrate` (`scripts/calibrate.ts`). Fixed seeds, so a re-run reproduces this file exactly.

Scope: every pair of the 21 RoboArena policies with at least 30 paired sessions: **29 pairs, 15,560 paired observations**. Gate settings: α = 0.05, superiority, rollback margin 0.02.

## (a) False decisions when there is no real difference

Each real paired difference has its sign flipped at random (the same as swapping which arm is baseline in that session), so the true mean is exactly 0 and any declared winner is wrong. 400 replications per pair. "Naive" runs a two-sided t-test every 10 pairs and stops at the first p < 0.05. "Confidence sequence" counts any point where the interval excludes 0, which includes every false PROMOTE or ROLLBACK the gate could make.

| | False-decision rate | 95% interval | Replications |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naive peeking | **29.2%** | 28.4% – 30.0% | 11,600 |
| Confidence sequence | **0.94%** | 0.78% – 1.13% | 11,600 |

Target: at most 5%. Highest single-pair confidence-sequence rate: 3.0% (paligemma_binning_droid → paligemma_fast_droid, n = 677). Pairs above 5%: 0 of 29 (with 400 replications a single pair's rate has a standard error of about 1 percentage point).

The confidence sequence is conservative here: it uses about 1% of a 5% error budget. That slack is the price of being valid at every look, and it is why the gate holds on small effects that a fixed test sized for them would detect (section b).

**What this does not test:** the guarantee assumes the mean difference is constant over time. Sign flipping keeps the real magnitudes and their order but forces the mean to 0, so drift in the mean (evaluator, site or date effects changing over time) is outside this check.

## (b) Time to decision vs a fixed-sample test

The gate replays each pair's real sessions in time order and stops at its first decision. A fixed-sample test must commit to a size before any data arrive: n = ((z₁₋α/₂ + z₀.₈) σ / MDE)², two-sided α = 0.05, 80% power. σ is each pair's observed standard deviation, which is generous to the fixed test (a planner would only have an estimate). The gate reached a decision on **13 of 29** pairs; the rest ran out of data (HOLD).

| MDE chosen in advance | Median fixed-sample n | Fixed test fits in the data | Fixed test finds a difference | Gate decided before the fixed n | Median gate n (decided) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 453 | 21 of 29 | 15 of 21 | 12 of 29 | 61 |
| 0.075 | 202 | 21 of 29 | 12 of 21 | 8 of 29 | 61 |
| 0.1 | 114 | 21 of 29 | 11 of 21 | 7 of 29 | 61 |
| 0.125 | 73 | 28 of 29 | 14 of 28 | 7 of 29 | 61 |
| 0.15 | 51 | 28 of 29 | 12 of 28 | 3 of 29 | 61 |

"Fixed test finds a difference" runs the planned two-sided t-test once, on the first n sessions in time order. "Gate decided before the fixed n" counts all 29 pairs; a pair the gate never decided counts as not faster.

Reading it: a fixed test sized for a small effect is slow whatever the truth turns out to be; one sized for a large effect is fast but misses smaller real differences. The gate needs no MDE, adapts to the effect that is actually there, and can stop early in either direction. The price is that it needs more pairs than a fixed test sized for exactly the right effect.
